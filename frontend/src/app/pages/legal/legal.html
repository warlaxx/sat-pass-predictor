Using the website or the API means accepting these terms. If you do not accept them, do not use the service.

The service

NextPass computes when satellites pass over a place on Earth, from public orbital elements and the SGP4 model. The website is free to use. The API is available with a key, under the quotas of the chosen plan.

Predictions are estimates

Times and positions come from a model fed with elements published hours or days earlier, and their accuracy degrades with that age. The methodology gives the orders of magnitude. They are suitable for observation, photography, amateur radio and planning. They must not be used for any purpose where an error could endanger people or property, such as collision avoidance, navigation or safety of flight.

Using the predictor

  • No account needed. The predictor itself sets no cookie and stores nothing in your browser; only the advertising below may, and in Europe only with your consent.
  • The coordinates you enter are sent to the prediction API to compute the passes, and appear in the page address so a result can be shared. They are not written to any database; an identical query may be answered from memory for up to five minutes.
  • "Use my position" asks your browser, only when you press it. The position is rounded to about ten metres and used only to fill the form.
  • Fonts are served by this site, not by a third party, so loading the page does not send your address to a font provider.
  • Photographs of space objects come from Wikimedia Commons and are loaded through this site's servers, not directly by your browser from Wikimedia.
  • Cloud forecasts are asked of MET Norway by this site's servers, not by your browser: they send it the place of the passes rounded to a tenth of a degree (about 11 km), never your exact position. The forecast for that rounded place is kept in memory, not in a database, and nothing ties it to you.
  • Audience measurement uses Vercel Web Analytics, which counts page views without cookies and without identifying visitors.
  • On the separation pages, a few actions (opening an event, a table or a fragment's page, using your position, opening a pass) each add one to a counter for the day. Nothing else is sent or kept: no cookie, no identifier, no address, not your position.
  • Feedback by e-mail is optional. A message you send, with the address of the page it was written from filled in for you, is used only to answer and improve the site, never added to a mailing list. to be completed: how long messages are kept
  • Advertising is served by Google AdSense, which chooses where ads appear and receives your IP address to deliver them. In the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland, Google asks for your consent before using cookies or personal data for ads, and you can change that choice at any time. Google explains how it uses data from the sites that show its ads.

API accounts

  • Sign-in is through GitHub. The account keeps your GitHub numeric identifier, not your name, email address or repositories.
  • API keys are stored only as a SHA-256 hash: nobody, including the publisher, can read a key back.
  • Usage counters record how many requests each key made per UTC day, to enforce quotas.
  • Payments, when open, are handled by Stripe. The account keeps a Stripe customer identifier; card details never reach this service.
  • Cookies on the account page are limited to the sign-in session and its CSRF protection, which the page cannot work without; they therefore need no consent.

E-mail reminders

  • What is kept: your e-mail address, the satellite, the place rounded to a hundredth of a degree (about a kilometre), your thresholds, your time zone and language, and the date of the last reminder. Nothing else: no name, no IP address, no record of opened e-mails.
  • Double opt-in. Nothing is sent until you press "Confirm my reminders" on the page the confirmation e-mail links to. A sign-up not confirmed within 48 hours is deleted, address included.
  • Sending goes through Resend, which receives your address and the text of each e-mail to deliver it. The cloud forecast is asked of MET Norway for the rounded place, never with your address.
  • Unsubscribing takes one click from any reminder and deletes the subscription and its address at once.
